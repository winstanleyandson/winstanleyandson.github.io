## What the video covers

Nate Herk compares two new personal-agent products: OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse. Both are always-on assistants that live in the cloud, have their own virtual computer, keep a memory, run scheduled tasks and make documents, slides and dashboards. He spent about a day with each. His view is that Dots is the more capable product underneath (a stronger model, a direct link to Codex and Slack) but that it feels rushed and buggy, while Muse has, in his view, the better interface, a free tier, editable memory and a WhatsApp connection. He covers memory, connectors, pricing, the models behind each, and walks through both apps screen by screen. He ends by choosing Dots for himself because he already works inside Codex and Claude Code, while saying he would point a non-technical friend or relative to Muse. He expects products of this kind to converge, so that the real differences will be connections, model quality and price. It is a product review, not a build.

**6:58 Pricing and Model Performance.** Herk says Muse's free tier gives up to 100 million tokens a week, and he has not paid for it. **10:57 Dots Interface and Limitations.** Dots is plain by comparison. Its one clear advantage is voice: you can call it and it replies quickly. It has saved logins, a terminal view and files. He could not manage scheduled tasks inside the Dots screen and had to find them in the Codex scheduled list. When Dots started Codex threads, they landed in recents and not in his project, so his organisation broke. Dots also described its own features wrongly, which cost him trust.

**13:02 My Verdict and Final Thoughts.** He would choose Dots because he lives in Codex and Claude Code, and would pick Muse if starting fresh. ## Relevance to Winstanley & Son

- **The shop already has this pattern, built to its own rules.** The reception agent in Slack, the response handler and the finance agent already do what these products offer in general form: staff talk in plain English, the agent acts, buttons confirm. Nothing here suggests replacing them with a consumer assistant.
- **Slack as the front door is the useful idea.** The estate already uses Slack for staff, alerts and status. The video reinforces that a team-facing agent in a channel is the pattern that sticks, but it adds nothing the estate lacks.
- **Approvals tab and heartbeat are worth noting for the AI CIO.** Muse shows pending approvals and scheduled jobs in one place and checks documents and email every 30 minutes. The estate has the watchdog and the owner-presses rule but no single screen of pending approvals and scheduled jobs. The AI CIO charter already plans registers of agents, so this would extend it as a register, not as a Meta product.
- **Editable memory and soul files match the existing approach.** The shop already keeps memory and a router file in plain text. Dots going stale from old chat memory supports keeping memory in files the owner can read and correct.
- **Privacy and patient data rule these out for patient work.** Both run as cloud agents, so memory sits with the vendor, and a Meta product linked to messaging apps does not fit the rule that patient data stays out of chat and that AI reading clinical records has no compliance approval. If either is tried, it is for non-patient tasks only, such as marketing ideas, with pseudonymised material.
- **Wait and watch.** Herk expects these products to converge and Dots to be buggy now. The owner's own notes favour proving a thing in the test channel before staff see it, so a trial would sit behind the change gate and not replace any live system.

## Account or role ideas this video suggests

1. **Approvals and Schedules Board** (reports to the AI CIO) [8:36]. Mission: one page listing everything waiting for the owner and every scheduled agent with its last run. Inputs: Make scenario status, watchdog output, pending drafts. Outputs: a weekly page and a daily "waiting on you" line. Guardrails: lists, never approves; no patient names.
2. **Heartbeat Reader** (AI CIO) [8:36]. Mission: a 30-minute check on administrative mail not already handled by the finance agent or response handler (for example insurer or authority letters) and key documents, for anything unanswered. Outputs: a short Slack alert. Guardrails: administrative headers and counts only, never clinical content; read-only.
3. **Team Channel Concierge** (owner) [4:42]. Mission: staff ask a question in a Slack channel and get a sourced answer from the shop's own written procedures (distinct from the reception agent, which handles orders and updates). Inputs: procedure notes, supplier rules. Outputs: threaded replies. Guardrails: no live writes; unsure answers say so; no patient data.
4. **Memory Custodian** (AI CIO) [3:08, 2:21]. Mission: keep the agents' memory and persona files current, dated and readable. Outputs: a monthly list of stale items for the owner to fix. Guardrails: owner edits; no secrets in files.
5. **Ideas Scout** (AI CMO; low priority) [9:22, 10:09]. Mission: turn the week's public marketing news into three questions for the owner. Outputs: a short Monday note. Guardrails: no spend, no posting.
