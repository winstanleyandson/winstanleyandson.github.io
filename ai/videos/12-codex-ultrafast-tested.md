## What the video covers

Nate Herk tests Ultrafast, a new speed setting in OpenAI's Codex. Per Herk, it works only with the GPT-6 Astra model and only on a new $500 a month plan (or through the API). It is marketed as eight times faster than standard and four times faster than the existing fast mode. Herk runs the same prompt twice, once on standard and once on Ultrafast, both at extra-high reasoning, and compares three things: time taken, quality of the result and how much of his weekly usage allowance each run burned. The first job is a 60-second vertical reel cut from a Google trip, using 145 GB of raw footage and two voice recordings. The second is a seven-page YouTube resource guide. The reel was only about 2.5 times faster but used about four times the allowance. The guide was closer to the advertised speed (about seven times) but still cost 3% of his allowance against 1%. His verdict is that it is genuinely fast, worth pulling only in a pinch, and that quality was not the reason to pay. It is a product test, not a build.

## What viewers said

Comments are opinion, not fact. About forty were collected, and many are short reactions.

- **Pushback on price and value.** The largest group says $500 a month is too much, that the allowance runs out within minutes, and that speed is not worth burning that much. A couple call it a gimmick aimed at influencers. One suggests the speed should start at a cheaper tier.
- **A factual claim to check.** One viewer says the $500 plan is the old $200 plan renamed with the same compute, and another says things seemed to slow down that week, implying lower plans were slowed. Neither is verified, and I did not search for either.
- **Method criticism.** One viewer asks why quality was compared when the model is the same and speed was the point. Another says nobody can tell which model or reasoning level produced either output, and that the results look like different effort. A third says a short test is rushed, and one preferred the standard reel as more coherent.
- **Speed benefit questioned.** Viewers ask whether a human can read output that fast and whether saving minutes justifies the usage.
- **Confirmations.** Some thank him for a plain, no-hype answer, and one likes the closing point that you only need it when pushed.
- **Off topic.** Requests for a Claude Code mods video, a Portuguese audio track and advice on a small flower business, plus one general attack on AI.

## Relevance to Winstanley & Son

- **Nothing here changes what is live.** The estate's slow steps are gates, not model speed: the owner presses activation, builds are proven in the isolated Slack test channel, and Codex reviews each package. A faster model would shorten drafting, not those gates, and Herk himself says he would probably never use the top speed.
- **The usage cost is the useful lesson.** A speed setting that turns a few per cent of a weekly allowance into a double-digit share is the kind of cost the AI CIO's registers should show. The estate's gaps list names registers that are not yet maintained files, so Herk's reel figures are a reminder to record allowance per job type before any premium mode is trialled.
- **The A/B method is reusable.** Same prompt, two settings, time and allowance recorded, outputs compared side by side. That fits the existing `agent-stress-test` skill and could settle any future model or setting change before staff see it. Judge with a fixed checklist, since Herk's preference rested on one viewing of each.
- **Reel and guide drafting is the one genuinely new area.** The business makes monthly batches of social cards and short videos and publishes long guides. An agent that drafts a vertical reel from approved workshop footage, or a guide from a script, is not listed as live. Staff or customer footage must stay in Draft for owner approval, and guides must keep the no-prices and no-volumes rules.
- **Do not buy it.** The video offers no evidence that a small independent practice gains from a $500 tier, and the claim that it is a repriced older plan is unverified. Treat as watch-only.

## Account or role ideas this video suggests

1. **AI Usage Meter** (reports to the AI CIO) [1:33, 5:39]. Mission: record how much of each plan's allowance each type of job consumes. Inputs: usage screens or exports, job type. Outputs: weekly line in the CIO pack with a flag for jobs over a set share. Guardrails: reads figures only; never changes plan or settings; no keys in output.
2. **Model and Mode Bench** (AI CIO) [4:52, 6:35]. Mission: run same-prompt trials of a new model or speed setting and report time, usage and a checklist score. Inputs: a fixed prompt set built from pseudonymised fixtures. Outputs: a one-page verdict. Guardrails: fixtures only; no live systems; owner decides adoption.
3. **Reel Rough-Cut Drafter** (AI CMO) [1:09]. Mission: draft a 60-second vertical reel from approved footage and a voice note. Inputs: a folder the owner has cleared and a brief. Outputs: a draft file held in Draft. Guardrails: no patients on screen without recorded consent; owner approves before posting; no prices or volumes.
4. **Guide Drafter** (AI CMO) [5:12, 6:35]. Mission: turn a source video or notes into a guide with internal links and source links. Outputs: a draft page for owner review. Guardrails: eye-test interval wording stays clinical (up to yearly for certain groups, optometrist decides); no contact-lens content.
5. **Meeting Recap Clerk** (owner) [0:00]. Mission: summarise the team's own meeting transcripts into decisions and actions. Guardrails: staff meetings only, never patient calls or records, since AI reading clinical records has no approval.
